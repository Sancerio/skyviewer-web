# SkyViewer competitive research

**Research date:** 2026-09-15 (Asia/Singapore)  
**Scope:** first party product/support pages and the official Stellarium Web Engine source. Features and catalog sizes can change with app versions or subscription tiers; each claim below is dated by this research pass.

## Executive findings

The five products establish the same useful loop: choose a location and time, point or pan the sky, search for a target, follow a directional cue, and open an information card. Their “AR” descriptions are camera video with a computed sky layer. Sky Guide says it “superimposes figures” on the real sky; Star Walk 2 says it overlays the sky map on the camera view; Stellarium Mobile describes blending the sky with the device camera; SkyView describes camera AR and gyroscope calibration. None of the reviewed first party sources claims that the camera recognizes stars from image pixels. **Inference:** a SkyViewer browser implementation should describe its first release as a calculated overlay driven by catalog, ephemeris, location, time, and device pose. Do not promise camera image recognition.

The strongest browser product baseline is permission-independent: the map, search, details, time travel, and manual drag must work when camera, motion, compass, GPS, or geolocation is unavailable. For the current SkyViewer milestone, rear-camera AR is a **P0 release capability** as an explicit opt-in enhancement, with a visible accuracy/calibration state and a complete map fallback.

SkyViewer's bundled star layer is finite: 5,044 Hipparcos stars through visual magnitude 6, plus the bundled name/identifier data described in [DATA.md](DATA.md). “All stars” therefore means all 5,044 bundled entries are searchable and have the facts available in this dataset; it does not mean every astronomical star or parity with Stellarium's billion-star-scale catalog. The current catalog work should finish alias/identifier indexing (proper name, Bayer, Flamsteed, HIP, and constellation) and richer object facts, while showing unknown fields as unknown.

## Product-specific observations

### SkyView: low-friction orientation and offline-first messaging

Terminal Eleven’s product page documents a 24-hour sky track for any star, planet, or satellite, a search arrow, date changes, 88 constellations, star visibility controls, night mode, and gyroscope calibration. Its current Android listing adds the clearest current scope statement: camera AR, galaxies/stars/constellations/planets/satellites, thousands of facts, and no Wi-Fi/data/GPS requirement. The support page explains the operational failure mode: magnetic interference causes pointing error, and figure-eight reset or moving away from metal may be needed. This is a good model for a persistent “calibrate / map still works” path.

The free/paid split is also a warning against vague “all stars” language. The official support page says the free version has a small star subset and 15 popular constellations, while the paid version adds all 88 constellations, more stars and galaxies, all planets, and brighter satellites. SkyViewer should state its catalog cutoff and dynamic-data coverage instead of implying that every visible point is available.

### Sky Guide: best documented recovery UX

Sky Guide’s support documentation is unusually concrete about the failure path. Motion tracking is enabled before the AR button appears; the user can move two fingers vertically to adjust the blend against the sky; a touch-and-hold gesture exposes Compass Correction and applies a left/right offset for the current session. The troubleshooting article also tells users to check location, date/time, magnetic cases, and figure-eight calibration. The object finder supports typed search and category browsing across constellations, stars, planets, and deep-sky objects, then an arrow guides the user.

The App Store listing (current version history reaches 13.0) describes detailed descriptions and rich graphics, cinematic time controls, and operation without Wi-Fi, cellular service, or GPS; the support article narrows the last point by recommending manual location on devices without GPS. SkyViewer should copy the behavior pattern—explain the cause, offer a correction, retain the session offset—not copy the visual design or wording.

### Star Walk 2: rich education, but freshness is part of the data contract

Vito documents three distinct modes: free roam/manual scrolling, Star Spotter movement following, and camera AR. Its product page couples object discovery with Time Machine, Planet Walk, visible-tonight planning, object facts (including mass, brightness, and distance), and 3D constellation models. The current premium page qualifies the catalog: thousands of deep-sky objects, small bodies, interactive models, and 10,000+ satellites are additional content.

The support page says satellite and comet paths require an internet connection for current data, because satellite orbits change and are only shown for a short window around the current date. The official compass tutorial says to grant location, figure-eight calibrate, then manually drag a bright object into alignment if needed; because of technical restrictions, the user may need to repeat this each time the app opens. This supports a SkyViewer rule: show the data timestamp/source and distinguish stable bundled stars from refresh-dependent satellites/comets.

### Stellarium Mobile: catalog breadth and sensor mode are separate concerns

Stellarium Labs publishes the clearest scale comparison. The online-capable product advertises Gaia DR2 with over 1.4 billion stars, over 3 million nebulae and galaxies, planets and natural satellites, comets, over 10,000 asteroids, artificial satellites, imagery, and sky cultures. It says the app works without internet using a reduced 2.5 million-star, 2.9 million-DSO, 10,000-asteroid set. The same page says the app can show any time/place and uses sensor control to identify a star by pointing.

The FAQ gives the sensor usability details: Sensor Mode needs a magnetometer/compass, should be set to Auto, and works best with the back of the device aimed at the sky away from magnets or metal. The search update documents visibility and instrument filters, useful for separating “computed above horizon” from “likely visible to the naked eye.” The AR announcement describes camera blending as a separate second step after sensor mode. SkyViewer should keep manual map navigation and sensor pointing independent so camera permission is never a prerequisite for sky calculation.

### Stellarium Web: strongest engine evidence, weakest product contract for AR

Stellarium Labs describes Stellarium Web as an online planetarium in a browser and explicitly labels it beta. The official Web Engine repository is more useful for architecture: it is a JavaScript/WebGL renderer with Gaia access over one billion stars, HiPS surveys, high-resolution planet textures, constellations, layers/shapes, and landscapes. The embeddable sample configures local data sources for stars, western sky culture, DSOs, minor planets, planets, comets, and satellites; its selection card renders magnitude, RA/Dec, and Az/Alt. The observer structure stores geographic coordinates and multiple times (TT, UT1, UTC).

The reviewed public product sources do not document camera AR, device orientation permission, or an offline guarantee. Treat the public site as an online planetarium reference and the engine as a separate technical dependency. The sample’s header and repository license identify AGPL v3 or an alternative commercial license; perform a license review before embedding it in a differently licensed project. A smaller independent catalog plus Astronomy Engine is easier to bound for SkyViewer v0.1.

## Camera AR versus sensor pointing

### Two different experiences

1. **Planetarium sensor pointing:** calculate object positions from catalog/ephemeris, observer location, and time; use compass/gyroscope/accelerometer orientation to rotate the rendered sky. No camera is needed. The user sees a simulated sky and can still pan manually.
2. **Camera AR:** render the same calculated sky as a transparent layer over a camera video stream. The camera supplies visual context; it does not establish celestial truth. A heading/elevation/roll estimate and calibration offset establish alignment. Without an image-recognition model, a bright pixel in the camera frame is not independently identified.

The first party descriptions support this distinction: Sky Guide describes superimposed figures and blend/correction controls; Star Walk 2 describes an overlaid sky map; Stellarium Mobile describes blending the sky with the camera after Sensor Mode; SkyView describes camera AR plus gyroscope calibration. **Inference:** our UI should say “calculated overlay” or “pointing assist,” show the catalog/ephemeris result, and reserve “camera recognition” for a future separately validated capability.

### Browser constraints (W3C, retrieved 2026-09-15)

The [W3C Device Orientation and Motion specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/orientation-event/) is a 2025 Candidate Recommendation Draft, not a final guarantee of interoperable behavior. It defines orientation as high-level alpha/beta/gamma rotations. Relative orientation uses accelerometer and gyroscope against an arbitrary reference, so it conveys changes from the initial position. Absolute orientation uses accelerometer, gyroscope, and magnetometer against the Earth reference frame. The specification makes accelerometer, gyroscope, and magnetometer policy-controlled permissions; absolute events require all three, and `deviceorientationabsolute` is marked at risk. Interfaces are restricted to secure contexts, and `requestPermission(true)` requires transient user activation and additionally requests magnetometer access.

The [W3C Media Capture and Streams specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/mediacapture-streams/) defines `navigator.mediaDevices.getUserMedia({video: ...})` as a permissioned camera request. It can reject when the user denies permission, the document is not allowed to use the camera, the page is not active, or the selected constraints/device cannot be satisfied. Camera use must remain visible to the user and stop cleanly when AR is exited.

**Implementation consequence:** use HTTPS, feature-detect every sensor/camera API, request motion/orientation from a user click or tap, request camera only when AR is enabled, report `unsupported`, `denied`, `relative`, or `absolute` states, and always retain drag/keyboard/touch navigation. The browser can provide best-effort pose input; it cannot guarantee native-app-grade heading accuracy across devices. Screen orientation and the API’s portrait-based device coordinate frame must be handled explicitly. Physical iOS Safari and Android Chrome checks are required; simulated sensor events are useful for unit tests but do not certify real alignment.

## Prioritized product principles

### P0: current camera release

- **Permission-independent sky:** app shell, catalog, map, search, object details, time travel, and manual drag work with camera/motion/location permissions denied or absent.
- **Camera AR is release scope:** the rear-camera overlay starts only after explicit user action, exposes its pose/calibration state, handles landscape and crop, and preserves the map when camera or motion input fails.
- **Computed truth is explicit:** every position is tied to UTC, observer coordinates, coordinate frame, catalog/ephemeris source, and a visible/above-horizon state. Do not imply that camera pixels were recognized.
- **Finite catalog search is complete:** all 5,044 bundled mag-6 Hipparcos stars and their available proper-name, Bayer, Flamsteed, HIP, and constellation identifiers are searchable; results expose the available facts and never invent missing metadata. This is a bounded local catalog, not a claim of billion-star parity.
- **Search leads to action:** name, alias, designation, and constellation search returns a selectable object, centers it, shows a directional arrow or bearing/elevation delta, and opens details.
- **Recovery is a product feature:** manual location, “Now,” drag/zoom, compass calibration offset, reset offset, and a clear “sensor unavailable/relative only” status are always reachable.
- **No false visibility:** show negative altitude/below-horizon warnings and distinguish “above geometric horizon” from naked-eye visibility or local obstruction.
- **Data provenance and freshness:** ship a pinned catalog with attribution; label network-updated satellites/comets separately from static stars; keep source/version information inspectable.
- **Independent visual identity:** use original typography, controls, icons, copy, and constellation artwork. Competitor behavior is a usability reference, not an asset source.

### P1: follow-on scope

- Visible-tonight and rise/set/transit summaries, a timeline with date/time entry, playback speed, and a one-tap return to Now.
- Layer controls for constellation lines/names, grids, magnitude cutoff, night mode, and deep-sky symbols; object cards with designation, type, magnitude, coordinates, current altitude/azimuth, and educational description.
- A catalog filter for naked-eye/binocular/telescope use, modeled on Stellarium Mobile’s search filters, without claiming that an above-horizon object is observable under local light pollution or weather.
- **Conditional future offline/PWA:** if offline packaging is later shipped, specify cache versioning, data size, and satellite/comet freshness behavior first. It is not a gate for the current camera milestone.

## Browser camera AR acceptance criteria

A current camera AR milestone is complete when criteria 1–7 and 9 are demonstrable. Criterion 8 is conditional future/P1 acceptance only if offline packaging is shipped; it is not a current camera milestone gate.

1. **Map fallback:** on desktop and mobile, with camera and motion denied, unsupported, or revoked, the map remains usable; search, selection, details, time, location presets/manual coordinates, drag, and zoom remain functional.
2. **Permission UX:** the AR button is the only entry point; `requestPermission(true)` is called only from its user gesture when available; camera is requested only after AR intent; denied/blocked/unsupported outcomes produce a concise status and a manual fallback.
3. **Pose semantics:** UI labels relative versus absolute orientation and never displays an absolute-north claim for relative data. Tests cover portrait and landscape, `alpha` wraparound, null readings, screen rotation, and event loss.
4. **Alignment and calibration:** a test catalog target at known azimuth/altitude projects to the expected screen direction from mocked pose data within the agreed numerical tolerance; users can apply, see, reset, and temporarily retain a session correction offset. Physical iOS Safari and Android Chrome checks record the target, time, location, device, permission state, correction, and observed error.
5. **Camera lifecycle:** the rear camera stream is visibly indicated, the overlay is composited above the video without claiming occlusion or image recognition, and all tracks stop when AR exits, the page hides, or permission is revoked.
6. **Astronomy state:** changing UTC or observer location moves computed planets and updates object details; static-star positions use the documented epoch/propagation path; below-horizon targets stay selectable but are marked unavailable for the current view.
7. **Search/details coverage:** every bundled object name and alias is searchable; every result exposes stable identifier, type, source, position, magnitude or brightness field when available, and current computed altitude/azimuth; missing metadata is shown as unknown rather than invented.
8. **Conditional offline and freshness:** if offline packaging is enabled, the static map/catalog loads without network; if dynamic satellite/comet data are unavailable or stale, the UI states the last update/fallback and does not present old orbital data as current.
9. **Accessibility and safety:** all controls have labels and keyboard/touch equivalents; night mode does not hide status; no permission is required at startup; camera/motion can be disabled without losing the core experience.

## Evidence limits and unresolved questions

- Product marketing and support pages do not expose complete catalog schemas, astrometric accuracy, or a universal feature matrix by plan/device. Recheck current app builds before making parity promises.
- Star Walk 2’s official material has a historical “no internet” manual statement and a current support statement requiring internet for fresh satellite/comet paths; the latter should govern current freshness UX.
- Stellarium Web’s static official page confirms an online beta product, while the open-source engine demonstrates broad capabilities. It does not establish that the hosted site supports the same data sources, offline packaging, or sensor AR in every browser.
- Browser sensor and camera standards define permission and data contracts, not heading accuracy. Hardware interference, magnetic cases, calibration, browser policy, and screen orientation remain physical QA concerns.
